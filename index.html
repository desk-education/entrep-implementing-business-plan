Section 1

The 6 Core Operational Pillars

Understanding the roadmap from concept validation to sustainable execution.

1

Concept Validation & Market Definition

Identify specific target demographics. Define your unique Value Proposition and launch a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) to gather real customer feedback with minimal risk.

2

Business Model & Financial Basics

Structure fixed and variable costs. Determine pricing to ensure positive gross margins and maintain strict cash flow management to prevent timing gaps between payables and receivables.

3

Legal & Entity Requirements

Select an appropriate legal structure (Sole Proprietorship, LLC, Partnership). Secure permits and separate personal funds from business bank accounts to protect personal assets.

4

Core Operational Setup

Establish reliable supply chains, deploy merchant payment gateways (Stripe, Square, PayPal), and integrate accounting and CRM software for consistent operations.

5

Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy

Acquire initial clients via direct outreach, local SEO (Google Business Profile), organic content, customer reviews, and low-budget targeted advertising.

6

Execution & Key Performance Indicators

Monitor essential metrics: Gross Profit Margin, Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Customer Lifetime Value (LTV), and monthly Burn Rate / Runway.

Section 5

Pedagogical Analysis & Guiding Questions

Deconstruct instructional strategies and clarify key understandings.

1. Constructivist Alignment: Activates existing schema by connecting new business jargon to familiar past realities (selling secondhand items, helping in family stores).

2. Dyadic Interactions: Low-stakes peer sharing provides psychological safety, letting students verbalize latent knowledge before whole-class participation.

3. Visual Scaffolding: Categorizing student input directly translates informal terms into formal pillars: Value Proposition, Cost Structures, and Revenue Margins.

Reflective Question 1

"How did your past experience of buying or selling reveal hidden costs—like time, effort, or transport—that people often forget when setting a price?"

Reflective Question 2

"Looking back at your story, what made your customer willing to give you their hard-earned money instead of keeping it or going elsewhere?"

Topic Transition Question

"If taking one simple transaction requires balancing value, costs, and customer trust, what formal steps and systems do we need to put in place to repeat that process successfully thousands of times over?"

Group Real-Life Application Work

Four focused teams executing Knowledge, Skills, Attitude, and Values objectives.

Group 1: Implementation Strategy Roadmap

Knowledge Objective
  • Outline Core Pillars: Create a 1-page roadmap for legal setup, financial metrics, and GTM.
  • Define Registration Steps: Pick legal structure (e.g., LLC) and outline required permits.
  • Set Benchmarks: Compute break-even sales and target gross margin.
Sample Pattern (Bean & Go Mobile Coffee)

Legal: LLC registration + municipal health permit.
Financials: Fixed = $500/mo, Price = $4.00, Variable = $1.00 $\rightarrow$ Break-Even = 167 cups.
GTM: "First 50 Cups Free" promo via local Instagram geo-tags during morning rush hour.

Group 4: Customer-Centric Care SOP (Maka-tao)

Values Integration

Design empathetic policies prioritizing human dignity and customer well-being above short-term profit.

"Make It Right" Service Policy Example

1. Instant Replacement: If coffee arrives cold or incorrect, replace it immediately for free or grant a full refund without hassle.

2. Inclusive Options: Stock oat and almond milk at $0 extra charge so dietary needs are respected equally.

3. Empathy Resolution Script: "We are so sorry for spoiling your morning routine! Here is your correct drink, plus a free drink voucher for tomorrow."

Teacher setup guide: Google Sheets + Apps Script

  1. Sign in to the school-approved Google account and create a new Google Spreadsheet. Give it a clear title, such as Grade 12 Entrepreneurship – Business Implementation Monitoring.
  2. Rename the first worksheet tab exactly StudentProgress. The script can also create this tab automatically if it is missing.
  3. The learning module is preconfigured to use the deployed Google Apps Script Web App endpoint shown above. The complete server-side Apps Script code is retained in the “Complete Google Apps Script Code (Code.gs)” panel below for authorized maintenance. You do not need to paste the endpoint into this HTML file or configure it separately on each device.
  4. The existing endpoint is already deployed. Do not create another deployment unless you are intentionally replacing or updating the server-side Apps Script project.
  5. In the existing Apps Script deployment, confirm that “Execute as” and “Who has access” follow your school and Google Workspace administrator's requirements. A publicly accessible deployment may expose the endpoint; do not use it for sensitive student records without authorization.
  6. Keep the existing deployment active and authorized. The permanent /exec URL is embedded in this HTML module and is used automatically by the synchronization and teacher-dashboard functions.
  7. On any device, enter the student's Full Name and Grade & Section, then click Synchronize Complete Dataset Now. The module automatically sends the complete dataset to the configured central Google Sheet; no endpoint pasting is required.
  8. Open the spreadsheet's StudentProgress tab. A new row should appear. Each synchronization appends a new snapshot row, so repeated submissions create a history of progress rather than overwriting earlier records.
  9. To synchronize future edits automatically on a device, tick Enable automatic Google Sheets synchronization on that device. The module waits briefly after changes before sending a complete snapshot. Keep the browser page open and connected to the internet while synchronization occurs. The endpoint itself is built into the HTML and is the same on every device.
  10. For the centralized monitoring dashboard, open Apps Script → Project Settings → Script Properties. Add a property named ADMIN_DASHBOARD_TOKEN and set a long, private random value. Do not put this token in the HTML source or share it with students.
  11. The complete Code.gs source displayed below already includes the doGet teacher-dashboard endpoint. If the deployed server-side project does not yet contain this version, update Code.gs and deploy a new version of the same Web App. The teacher enters the private token in the Centralized Teacher Monitoring Dashboard. The script returns the latest record per student name and grade/section, plus the number of saved snapshots. Keep the spreadsheet and deployment access restricted according to school policy.
  12. When you edit the Apps Script after deployment, open Deploy → Manage deployments, edit the existing deployment, select a new version, and deploy again. Editing the existing deployment normally preserves its /exec URL. If you create a different deployment with a new URL, update the GOOGLE_SHEETS_WEB_APP_URL constant in this HTML file to that new /exec URL.

Spreadsheet columns include: synchronization time, student identity, completion percentage, completed sections, latest quiz score, quiz-attempt history, blueprint/reflection JSON, and the full dataset JSON for later review or processing.

Developer Guide: Connect Donation Records to a Separate Google Sheet

Important: Use a separate spreadsheet and a separate Apps Script Web App deployment for donations. This keeps donor records independent from student learning-progress records.

  1. Sign in to the Google account that will own the donation records. Create a new Google Spreadsheet, for example Educational Website – GCash Donation Records. Keep its sharing permissions restricted to authorized administrators.
  2. In the new spreadsheet, choose Extensions → Apps Script. This opens a new Apps Script project bound to the donation spreadsheet.
  3. Delete the sample code in Code.gs, copy the complete donation Apps Script code from the code box below, and paste it into Code.gs. Save the project.
  4. Return to the spreadsheet and confirm that the worksheet tab is named exactly Donor Records. The script will create this tab and its column headings automatically if it does not exist.
  5. In Apps Script, select Deploy → New deployment. Select the gear icon and choose Web app. Set Execute as to your account. Choose the access setting permitted by your Google account and school/data-protection policy. The web app must accept the website's anonymous POST request for this simple public form to work; understand that anyone who obtains the endpoint may submit records.
  6. Click Deploy, authorize the script when prompted, and copy the Web App URL ending in /exec. Do not use the test /dev URL.
  7. In the HTML source, find GCASH_DONATION_WEB_APP_URL in the GCash Donation Feature script near the bottom. Replace only PASTE_DONATION_WEB_APP_URL_HERE with your new /exec URL. Keep the URL inside the quotation marks. Do not replace the existing student-progress endpoint.
  8. Save and publish the updated HTML page. Open the donation modal, send a small test donation only if appropriate, enter a test donor name and amount, tick the confirmation box, and submit. Check the Donor Records worksheet for the new row.
  9. When the Apps Script code changes, choose Deploy → Manage deployments, edit the donation Web App deployment, select New version, and deploy. If you create a new deployment instead, replace the URL in the HTML again.
  10. Keep a manual review process. The browser sends the form in no-cors mode, so it cannot read the Apps Script response. The page can report that the request was sent, but it cannot truthfully confirm that Google Sheets saved the row. Verify the row in the spreadsheet.
Data columns: Timestamp, Donor Name, Donor Type, Organization / Group, Country, Amount, and Source. The script validates required donor fields, uses a script lock to reduce simultaneous-write collisions, and appends each donation record as a new row. This website submits individual donor records; the Country field is collected in the form.
